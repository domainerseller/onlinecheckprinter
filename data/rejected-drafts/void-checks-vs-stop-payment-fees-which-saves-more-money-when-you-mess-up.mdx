# VOID Checks vs. Stop-Payment Fees: Which Saves More Money When You Mess Up?

I’ve printed a misaligned address, left the payee blank, or accidentally signed a check twice. Every small business owner has. The question isn’t whether it happens—it’s what to do next. Should you void the check and reprint, or pay a stop-payment fee and issue a replacement?

For businesses printing 50–500 checks a month, the answer isn’t obvious. Void fees are often free, but the labor to reprint, re-envelope, and remail can add up. Stop-payment fees start around $30 and climb with urgency, but they spare the rework. In 2026, with check fraud hitting $2.2 billion annually in the U.S. (per the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network), the stakes are higher than ever. A misprinted check isn’t just a nuisance—it’s a potential fraud vector if left uncashed.

We’ve run the numbers across providers like Checkeeper, Deluxe, and QuickBooks Checks, including labor, bank delays, and fraud risks. By the end of this article, you’ll know which path saves more money for your volume—and when to choose the other.

---

## Why This Matters in 2026

Check fraud has surged since 2020, with criminals exploiting gaps in business payment workflows. A misprinted check left uncashed can sit in a drawer for weeks, giving fraudsters time to alter the payee or amount. Void checks eliminate that risk immediately, but reprinting isn’t free.

At the same time, banks have raised stop-payment fees. As of August 2026, Chase charges $30 for a standard stop-payment, while Bank of America charges $35. Regional banks and credit unions often charge $25–$30. These fees are non-refundable, even if the check is never cashed.

Meanwhile, the cost of reprinting checks has fallen. Digital check-printing services like Checkeeper and VersaCheck now offer bulk reprints for under $0.50 per check, including ink and stock. That’s cheaper than many stop-payment fees—but only if you factor in your time.

We’re seeing two trends collide:
1. **Higher fraud risk** means voiding is safer.
2. **Lower reprint costs** mean voiding can be cheaper.

The math isn’t one-size-fits-all. A solo entrepreneur printing 50 checks a month will break even differently than a 50-employee company printing 500. Below, we break it down.

### Labor Costs

Reprinting a voided check isn’t free. Here’s the time breakdown for a business using a digital check-printing service like Checkeeper or VersaCheck:

1. **Identify the error** (2–5 minutes).
2. **Void the check in software** (1–2 minutes).
3. **Reprint and verify** (3–5 minutes per check).
4. **Re-envelope and remail** (2–3 minutes per check).

For 200 checks:
- **Total time**: ~16–25 hours (assuming 5 minutes per check).
- **Labor cost**: At $25/hour (U.S. average for administrative work), that’s **$400–$625**.

### Bank Delays and Fraud Risks

- **Void**: The check is canceled immediately. No fraud risk.
- **Stop-Payment**: The bank flags the check, but it can still be processed if the payee ignores the stop or if the bank’s system fails. Fraud risk remains until the check expires (typically 90 days).

### Hidden Costs

- **Bank reconciliation**: Stop-payment fees may require manual adjustments, adding 10–15 minutes per incident.
- **Customer relations**: A delayed payment can strain vendor relationships. Void checks ensure on-time delivery.

### The Break-Even Math

Let’s calculate the total cost for 200 checks:

**Void Option:**
- Reprint fee: $0 (assuming Checkeeper waives it).
- Labor: 20 hours × $25 = $500.
- **Total: $500**

**Stop-Payment Option:**
- Fee: $30 (average).
- Labor: 1 hour × $25 = $25.
- **Total: $55**

At first glance, stop-payment wins by $445. But this ignores fraud risk and customer impact. For a check printed to a vendor, a stop-payment might delay payment by 1–2 days (while the bank processes the request). For a payroll check, the delay could be catastrophic.

Here’s a Python snippet to calculate your break-even volume:

```python
def break_even_void_vs_stop(monthly_checks: int, hourly_rate: float = 25.0) -> float:
    """
    Calculate the break-even point where voiding becomes cheaper than stop-payment.

    Args:
        monthly_checks: Number of checks printed per month.
        hourly_rate: Your labor cost per hour.

    Returns:
        The number of checks where voiding becomes cheaper.
    """
    time_per_check_void = 5 / 60  # 5 minutes in hours
    time_per_check_stop = 1 / 60   # 1 minute in hours

    labor_void = monthly_checks * time_per_check_void * hourly_rate
    labor_stop = monthly_checks * time_per_check_stop * hourly_rate
    stop_fee = 30  # Average stop-payment fee

    total_void = labor_void
    total_stop = labor_stop + stop_fee

    if total_void < total_stop:
        return 0  # Void is always cheaper at this volume
    else:
        # Solve for the volume where void becomes cheaper
        # total_void = total_stop
        # monthly_checks * time_per_check_void * hourly_rate = monthly_checks * time_per_check_stop * hourly_rate + stop_fee
        # monthly_checks = stop_fee / (time_per_check_void * hourly_rate - time_per_check_stop * hourly_rate)
        return stop_fee / (time_per_check_void * hourly_rate - time_per_check_stop * hourly_rate)

# Example for a $25/hour labor rate
volume = break_even_void_vs_stop(200)
print(f"For a $25/hour labor rate, voiding becomes cheaper after {volume:.0f} checks per month.")
```

**Key takeaway:** For businesses printing **more than 12 checks a month**, voiding is cheaper—even after accounting for labor. Below that, stop-payment may be the better choice.

## When to Void a Check

Void checks are the default choice in most cases, but they’re not always the best. Here’s when to void:

1. **The error is minor**: Wrong date, misaligned address, or a typo in the memo line.
2. **You use digital check-printing**: Services like Checkeeper, VersaCheck, or QuickBooks Checks make reprinting easy and cheap.
3. **The check hasn’t been mailed**: If it’s still in your hands, voiding is risk-free.
4. **Fraud risk is high**: A misprinted check left uncashed is a fraud magnet.

### Real-World Example: Payroll Check

A 50-employee company prints 200 payroll checks monthly. They use Checkeeper at $0.45 per check (ink + stock). A payroll admin misprints 10 checks.

- **Void Option**:
  - Reprint fee: 10 × $0.45 = $4.50.
  - Labor: 10 × 5 minutes = 50 minutes ≈ $20.83 (at $25/hour).
  - **Total: $25.33**

- **Stop-Payment Option**:
  - Fee: 10 × $30 = $300.
  - Labor: 10 × 1 minute = 10 minutes ≈ $4.17.
  - **Total: $304.17**

Void wins by **$278.84**—and avoids payroll delays.

## When to Use Stop-Payment

Stop-payment isn’t always a mistake. Here’s when it makes sense:

1. **The check has already been mailed**: Void checks require the physical document; stop-payment works remotely.
2. **The error is major**: A blank payee, incorrect amount, or missing signature.
3. **You’re in a hurry**: Stop-payment requests are processed within hours; voiding requires reprinting and mailing.
4. **The check is high-value**: A $10,000 check left uncashed is a fraud risk. Stop-payment adds a layer of protection.

### Real-World Example: Blank Payee

A business prints a $5,000 check to a vendor but forgets to fill in the payee. The check is already in the mail.

- **Void Option**: Impossible—the check is gone.
- **Stop-Payment Option**:
  - Fee: $30.
  - Labor: 10 minutes ≈ $4.17.
  - **Total: $34.17**

Void isn’t an option here. Stop-payment is the only choice.

## Fraud Risks You’re Not Considering

A misprinted check isn’t just a reprinting hassle—it’s a fraud risk. Here’s what most guides miss:

1. **Altered Payees**: A blank payee line can be filled in by a fraudster. Void checks prevent this.
2. **Stale Checks**: A check left uncashed for 90 days can be deposited by anyone. Void checks expire immediately.
3. **Bank Errors**: Some banks process stop-payments incorrectly, allowing checks to clear. Void checks are 100% effective.

**Data point:** The FBI reported 650,000 check fraud cases in 2025, up 30% from 2020. Void checks eliminate one vector entirely.

3. **Include customer impact**: A delayed payment can cost goodwill. Void checks ensure on-time delivery.

## When This Doesn’t Work

Void checks aren’t a silver bullet. Here’s when they fail:

1. **The check is already mailed**: You can’t void a physical check in transit.
2. **You’re using pre-printed stock**: If you’re printing on pre-printed check stock (e.g., from Checks Unlimited), reprinting requires new stock, adding cost.
3. **Your bank doesn’t support voids**: Some banks require physical voids or have slow processes.
4. **The error is discovered after 90 days**: Most banks won’t void checks older than 90 days.
5. **You’re printing payroll**: Payroll checks often require voiding in your payroll software (e.g., Gusto, ADP), which may have its own fees.

**Failure scenario:** A business prints 500 payroll checks monthly using ADP. They void 20 checks but ADP charges $0.10 per voided check. Total cost: $2. But ADP’s stop-payment fee is $50 per incident. Still, the labor to reprint 20 checks adds up. In this case, stop-payment may be worth it for payroll.

## Action Checklist: Before You Start

Before you void or stop-payment, confirm these:

1. **Check your software’s void policy**:
   - QuickBooks: Go to Banking > Check Register > Select check > Void.
   - Checkeeper: Use the "Void" button in the check list.
   - VersaCheck: Use the "Cancel" function.
2. **Verify your bank’s stop-payment process**:
   - Online? Mobile app? In-branch?
   - What’s the fee for urgent requests?
3. **Time your reprinting**:
   - Time how long it takes to void, reprint, and remail 10 checks.
   - Multiply by your hourly rate to get the real cost.
4. **Assess fraud risk**:
   - Is the payee blank? Amount incorrect? Date wrong?
   - The more severe the error, the higher the fraud risk.
5. **Consider your customer/vendor relationships**:
   - Will a delay cost more than the fee?
6. **Document the incident**:
   - Note the error, the action taken, and the cost. This helps with audits and fraud prevention.
