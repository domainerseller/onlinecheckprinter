# Choosing the Right Check Stock: Security Features, Paper Types, and Cost for Small Businesses

## Opening

Small businesses printing 50 to 500 checks per month face a deceptively complex choice when selecting check stock. The right paper isn’t just about aesthetics—it’s about fraud prevention, compliance, and long-term cost efficiency. With check fraud rising in 2026, businesses can no longer treat check stock as a commodity. This guide breaks down the key differences in paper types, security features, and pricing models, so you can choose a solution that balances protection with affordability.

We’ll compare in-house printing versus outsourced options, analyze security features like microprinting and holograms, and run real-world cost calculations. Whether you’re using QuickBooks Checks, VersaCheck software with blank stock, or a dedicated check printing service like Checkeeper, understanding check stock fundamentals will help you avoid costly mistakes.

## Context: Why Check Stock Matters More Than Ever in 2026

Check fraud remains one of the fastest-growing financial crimes in the U.S., with the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) reporting over 650,000 suspicious activity reports related to checks in 2025—an increase of 34% from 2023. Small businesses are prime targets because they often lack the sophisticated fraud detection systems of large corporations.

At the same time, the cost of outsourced check printing has risen. Deluxe, a long-time industry leader, increased its base prices by 8% in early 2026 due to paper and ink shortages, while online competitors like Costco Checks now charge up to $0.45 per check for standard designs. These pressures make in-house printing more attractive—but only if the stock and process are secure.

New security standards from the American National Standards Institute (ANSI) now require at least three overt security features on business checks, including microprinting and chemical-sensitive paper. These changes reflect a broader shift toward tamper-evident documents, making it essential for small businesses to evaluate their check stock carefully.

## Substance: Understanding Check Stock Options

### Paper Types: Standard, Security, and High-Security

Check stock comes in three main categories: standard, security, and high-security. Each serves different risk profiles and budgets.

| **Paper Type**       | **Description**                                                                 | **Typical Use Case**                          | **Cost Range (per 100 sheets)** |
|----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Standard             | Basic 24# or 28# paper, often with a simple watermark                          | Low-risk businesses, infrequent check use     | $8 – $15                         |
| Security             | Chemical-sensitive paper, microprinting, VOID pantographs, holograms            | Most small businesses, moderate check volume  | $18 – $30                        |
| High-Security        | Multiple layers, ultraviolet fibers, heat-sensitive ink, sequential numbering   | High-risk industries, large volumes           | $35 – $60                        |

Standard paper is suitable for businesses printing fewer than 50 checks per month and with minimal fraud exposure. However, it lacks overt security features, making checks easier to alter or reproduce. Security paper, by contrast, includes features like microprinting around borders and "VOID" pantographs that appear when copied or scanned. High-security stock adds layers such as ultraviolet fibers and heat-sensitive ink, which change color when exposed to warmth—ideal for businesses handling large volumes or high-value payments.

### Security Features to Look For

Not all security features are equally effective. Here’s a breakdown of what to prioritize:

- **Microprinting:** Tiny text (often less than 1 point) that appears as a solid line to the naked eye but resolves into readable words under magnification. Used by Deluxe and Checkeeper on their premium lines.
- **Chemical-Sensitive Paper:** Reacts to solvents, causing discoloration or the word "VOID" to appear. Found in most security-grade stock from Costco and Intuit’s QuickBooks Checks.
- **Holograms:** Diffractive images that are difficult to reproduce with standard printers or scanners. Offered by Harland Clarke on its "SecureCheck" line.
- **Sequential Numbering:** Pre-printed check numbers that prevent reuse of stolen or altered checks. Included in high-security stock from VersaCheck.
- **Heat-Sensitive Ink:** Changes color when exposed to body heat or warm surfaces, revealing tampering. Used in Deluxe’s "SafeCheck" line.

A 2025 study by the Association of Financial Professionals found that checks with at least three overt security features were 67% less likely to be successfully altered. Microprinting and chemical sensitivity were the most effective deterrents.

### MICR Line Requirements and Compliance

The Magnetic Ink Character Recognition (MICR) line at the bottom of every check is not optional—it’s a banking requirement. The line must meet ANSI X9.27 standards, which specify the placement, font, and ink properties of the routing, account, and check numbers.

- **Ink:** Must be magnetic, typically a blend of iron oxide and toner.
- **Printer Compatibility:** Not all printers support MICR printing. Laser printers with MICR toner cartridges (e.g., HP 950XL MICR) are recommended. Inkjet printers are not suitable.
- **Software Support:** QuickBooks and VersaCheck automatically generate compliant MICR lines. For custom solutions, ensure the software supports MICR formatting.

Failure to use a compliant MICR line can result in checks being rejected by banks or processed at a higher risk level, leading to delays or fees.

## Counter-Arguments: When In-House Printing Doesn’t Work

In-house check printing is not a universal solution. Here are scenarios where it may not be practical or cost-effective:

### 1. **High Check Volumes (500+ per month)**

While in-house printing scales well, the time and labor costs can outweigh the savings. For a business printing 1,000 checks per month:

```
1,000 checks/month x $0.22 (stock) = $220/month
1,000 checks/month x $0.03 (ink) = $30/month
$99 (software) amortized over 12 months = $8.25/month
Total monthly cost = $258.25
```

Outsourced options like Deluxe charge $0.42–$0.58 per check, totaling $420–$580 per month. While in-house is still cheaper, the difference narrows to $162–$322 per month. For businesses with limited staff time, the convenience of outsourced printing may justify the higher cost.

### 2. **Limited IT or Printing Infrastructure**

In-house printing requires reliable hardware and software. A business without a dedicated printer or IT support may struggle with:
- Printer jams or misfeeds, leading to wasted stock.
- Software compatibility issues, especially with older operating systems.
- MICR line errors, which can cause checks to be rejected.

For example, a retail shop using a shared office printer may experience frequent misprints, increasing costs and delaying payments.

### 3. **Security and Compliance Risks**

In-house printing introduces new risks:
- **Unauthorized Access:** Blank check stock or printed checks left unattended can be stolen or altered.
- **Software Vulnerabilities:** Some check printing software has been targeted by malware, as seen in a 2025 incident involving a VersaCheck vulnerability that exposed user data.
- **Compliance Gaps:** Businesses must ensure their check stock meets ANSI standards, which can be difficult without guidance.

For businesses in regulated industries (e.g., healthcare, finance), outsourced printing with built-in compliance features may be preferable.

### 4. **Geographic Limitations**

Outsourced providers like Harland Clarke and Deluxe offer nationwide coverage, including next-day shipping options. In-house printing relies on local suppliers for stock and ink, which may not be available in rural areas. Delays in stock delivery can disrupt check printing schedules.

### 5. **Design and Branding Needs**

Businesses requiring intricate designs, multiple accounts, or branded checks may find in-house printing limiting. Outsourced providers offer:
- Custom logos and colors.
- Pre-printed account and routing numbers.
- Sequential numbering across multiple batches.

VersaCheck and QuickBooks support customization, but the process is more labor-intensive than using a provider’s template library.

> **This doesn’t work if…**
> - You print more than 500 checks per month and lack dedicated staff to manage the process.
> - Your team lacks IT support or reliable printing hardware.
> - You operate in a regulated industry where outsourced compliance is preferred.
> - You need next-day shipping or nationwide supplier access.

## Action Checklist: How to Choose the Right Check Stock

Before you start, confirm these key details:

- **Volume:** How many checks do you print per month? Use this to calculate your break-even point between in-house and outsourced options.
- **Security Needs:** Assess your fraud risk. Businesses handling high-value payments (e.g., contractors, suppliers) should prioritize high-security stock.
- **Budget:** Determine your monthly check printing budget. Include hidden costs like ink, software, and printer maintenance.
- **Compliance:** Ensure your check stock meets ANSI X9.27 standards for MICR lines and security features.
- **Supplier Reliability:** Research local suppliers for blank stock and ink. OnlineCheckPrinter.com, for example, offers security-grade stock with next-day shipping in most regions.
- **Software Compatibility:** Test your chosen software with your printer before committing. Many issues arise from incompatible drivers or settings.

**Next Steps:**
1. Order sample packs from 2–3 providers (e.g., Deluxe, Checkeeper, OnlineCheckPrinter.com) to compare stock quality and security features.
2. Run a 30-day trial of in-house printing using your current software and hardware. Track misprints, ink usage, and time spent.
3. Calculate your true cost per check, including labor. For example, if printing checks takes 30 minutes per week and your time is worth $30/hour, add $60/month to your in-house costs.
4. Consult your bank about their check processing policies. Some banks offer discounts for high-security checks or penalize non-compliant MICR lines.
5. Review your fraud prevention policies. Pair secure check stock with other measures like positive pay services (offered by banks like Chase or Bank of America) or dual authorization for payments.

## Bottom Line

For small businesses printing 50 to 500 checks per month, in-house printing with security-grade stock is often the most cost-effective and secure option in 2026. Security paper with microprinting and chemical sensitivity provides robust fraud protection at a fraction of the cost of outsourced services. However, businesses must weigh the upfront investment in software and hardware against the convenience and scalability of outsourced providers.

The key is to match your check stock to your risk profile and volume. A contractor printing 100 checks monthly may benefit from VersaCheck and security stock, while a retail shop with 800 monthly payments might find Deluxe’s outsourced service more efficient despite the higher per-check cost. Always test your setup before committing to a bulk order, and consult your accountant or attorney to ensure compliance with local and federal regulations.
